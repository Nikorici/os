Lab 13 - Semaphores in Your Own Kernel (xv6)
Daniel Nicorici, FAF-243. Full report with outputs: Lab13_DanielNicorici_FAF-243.pdf

1. One line costs 31 system calls: mix prints one letter per write(), 30 letters and the
   newline. Between any two of those calls the other process, running on another CPU, can
   make its own write(), so the letters of the two lines mix.

2. "sem_down(3);" before the inner loop and "sem_up(3);" after it. Semaphore 3 starts at 1,
   so only one process at a time can be between them, and the whole line becomes a critical
   section.

3. wakeup(s) wakes every process that sleeps on s, but sem_up added only one unit. The first
   process to get the spinlock takes it (value back to 0); the others wake up, find 0 and
   must sleep again. A process that calls sem_down without sleeping can also take the unit
   before a woken one gets the lock. "while" re-checks; "if" would let value go below 0.

4. A spinlock is held with interrupts off, and anyone else who wants it spins. If the process
   slept holding it, sem_up could never acquire s->lock to add 1 and call wakeup, so the
   sleeper would never wake, and the CPU calling sem_up would spin forever: a deadlock.

Optional sem_trydown was not done.
